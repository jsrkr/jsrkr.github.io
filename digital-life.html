About

The Atlas brings together three measured dimensions of digital life and summarizes them with the Digital Life Index. Figure 3 compares one state's normalized profile; Figure 4 shows selected underlying statistics for all states and DC in their original units.

Digital Work
The prevalence of working from home, measured using the American Community Survey.
Digital Leisure
Average daily time spent playing games and using computers or the internet for personal leisure, including recreational web browsing, social media, online chatting, and similar activities. Measured using the American Time Use Survey.
Digital Accessibility
Household access to the internet, high-speed internet, and cellular data plans, measured using the American Community Survey.

Method

The Digital Life Atlas combines information from the American Community Survey (ACS) and the American Time Use Survey (ATUS) to measure three dimensions of digital life across U.S. states: Digital Work, Digital Leisure, and Digital Accessibility.

Digital Work

Digital Work measures the prevalence of working from home using the American Community Survey.

Digital Leisure

Digital Leisure measures average daily time spent playing games and using a computer or the internet for personal leisure using the American Time Use Survey. Computer-based leisure includes activities such as recreational web browsing, social media use, online chatting, and other personal-interest computer use. It does not capture all forms of internet or screen use because ATUS classifies activities according to their primary purpose.

Digital Accessibility

Digital Accessibility combines household access to the internet, high-speed internet, and cellular data plans using the American Community Survey.

Index construction

The three dimensions are standardized and combined using principal component analysis (PCA). The first principal component is used to construct the overall Digital Life Index because it loads positively on all three dimensions.

How to interpret the scores

The resulting index is rescaled from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating greater digital-life intensity relative to other state-year observations in the sample.

The Digital Work Score, Digital Leisure Score, and Digital Accessibility Score displayed in Figures 1–3 are also normalized from 0 to 1 for ease of comparison over time. These are relative scores, not percentages. Figure 4 presents selected underlying measures—such as work-from-home shares, internet access, and average daily digital-leisure minutes—in their original units.

Coverage

The Atlas covers 2016–2019 and 2021–2023 for all 50 states and the District of Columbia. 2020 is omitted because the American Time Use Survey did not produce a comparable full-year estimate.
