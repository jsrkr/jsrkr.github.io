Digital Life Index

Digital life across U.S. states.

ABOUT

The Digital Life Index captures how digital technologies are embedded in everyday life: remote work, digital leisure (such as gaming, web browsing, and social media), and access to internet services. The extent to which our lives are digitalized can influence our wellbeing, social interaction, productivity, and family life. The data allow users to compare remote work, gaming and computer-based leisure, social media and web use, and household internet connectivity across U.S. states over time.

About the measures

The Digital Life Index captures three dimensions of digital exposure in everyday life: Digital Work, Digital Leisure, and Digital Accessibility. Together, these dimensions reflect how people engage with digital technologies through work, leisure, and access to digital infrastructure. The definitions below describe what each dimension measures.

Digital Work
The prevalence of working from home, measured using the American Community Survey.
Digital Leisure
Average daily time spent playing games and using computers or the internet for personal leisure, including recreational web browsing, social media, online chatting, and similar activities. Measured using the American Time Use Survey.
Digital Accessibility
Household access to the internet, high-speed internet, and cellular data plans, measured using the American Community Survey.

Method

Data and variables

The three dimensions are constructed using data from the American Community Survey (ACS) and the American Time Use Survey (ATUS).

The ACS provides information on the share of workers who work remotely and the share of households with internet access, high-speed broadband, and a cellular data plan. The ATUS provides information on digital leisure, including the average time spent on digital leisure activities and the share of individuals who engage in these activities. Figure 4 shows how these measures vary across states and over time.

Index construction

To place the three dimensions on a comparable scale, I first standardize each component. The standardized Digital Work, Digital Leisure, and Digital Accessibility scores are then combined using equal weights. Equal weighting reflects the objective of the index: to capture distinct dimensions of digital life without assigning greater importance to any one dimension.

The resulting index is rescaled to range from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate greater overall digital-life intensity. Figures 1–3 present the Digital Life Index and its component scores across states and over time.

How to interpret the scores

The Digital Life Index is a score ranging from 0 to 1. Higher scores indicate greater digital-life intensity, based on the combined levels of digital work, digital leisure, and digital accessibility.

For example, if State A has a Digital Life Index score of 0.75 and State B has a score of 0.40, State A has a higher level of digital-life intensity than State B. A score of 0.75 does not mean that 75% of the population is digitally engaged.

Coverage

The data cover 2016–2019 and 2021–2023 for all 50 states and the District of Columbia. 2020 is omitted because the American Time Use Survey did not provide a comparable full-year estimate for that year.
